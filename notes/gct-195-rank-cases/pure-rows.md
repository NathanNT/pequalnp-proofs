# Pure rows of a four-by-six level space

Let L be a complex linear space satisfying the four subpermanent-sum equations of L-gct-195. For each i, let Uᵢ consist of the vectors y for which the matrix supported on row i with row vector y belongs to L. If L contains (3f,3f,3f,−f) for any nonzero f, at most one Uᵢ has dimension greater than one. [Checker statement](PureRows195.txt) · [Lean proof](PEqualNP/Gct/PureRows195.lean).

Mixed coefficients force two distinct pure-row spaces to be orthogonal for the complex bilinear dot product. Both are orthogonal to 1, f, f² and f³, with powers taken coordinatewise. If both spaces had dimension at least two, the Gram determinants of the triples of powers with exponents (0,1,2) and (0,2,3) would vanish. These identities force the third and fifth moments to vanish, in addition to the first, second and fourth. Interpolation then gives a nonzero sixth moment. The dimension argument puts f² in both spaces, where a mixed quartic equation forces that moment to vanish. [Dimension argument](PEqualNP/Gct/PureGeometry.lean) · [Sixth moment](PEqualNP/Gct/PureSixth.lean) · [Assembly](PEqualNP/Gct/PureCriterion.lean).

The five fifth roots of unity with a sixth coordinate zero give a level line of the specified row profile. This example shows why a general kernel direction cannot simply be normalized to the three-coordinate cubic example. The dimension of the quotient by the pure rows remains an independent part of the problem. The [coefficient checks](check_pure_rows.py) accompany the [original mathematical note](pure-rows.fr.md).
